# Sources and mathematical context

The implementation's three-shear identity is a direct matrix identity and is
verified by the test suite. These sources provide established context for the
geometric concepts used to motivate the experiment.

1. Kelly, S. D. (1995), “Geometric phases and robotic locomotion,” *Journal of
   Robotic Systems*, 12(6), 417–431. DOI:
   https://doi.org/10.1002/rob.4620120607
   Introduces differential-geometric modelling of locomotion through cyclic
   changes in mechanism shape and associated geometric phase.

2. “Geometric phase predicts locomotion performance in undulating living
   systems across scales,” *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*
   (2024): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11181092/
   Describes shape-space loops, local connections and geometric phase in
   biological locomotion systems.

3. Hatton, R. L. and Choset, H. (2011), “Geometric motion planning: The local
   connection, Stokes’ theorem, and the importance of coordinate choice,”
   https://doi.org/10.1177/0278364910394392
   Discusses local connection vector fields and curvature for analysing
   displacement accumulated over cyclic shape changes.

4. UC Berkeley EECS, “Representing a Rotation as Three Shears”:
   https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~ug/slide/pipeline/assignments/as5/transforms3d.shtml
   Gives the three-shear factorization of a planar rotation.

5. University of Engineering and Technology, Taxila, “Transformations of
   Objects,” Chapter 5 notes:
   https://web.uettaxila.edu.pk/CMS/coeCGbsSp09/notes/Ece660Chap5.pdf
   Also presents a rotation as three successive shear matrices.

These references do not establish that the Virtual Layer itself has a natural
connection or that its codon space has biological or emotional resonance. That
is the hypothesis under investigation, not a result imported from the sources.
